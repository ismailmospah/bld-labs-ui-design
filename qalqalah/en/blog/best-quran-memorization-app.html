The Best App to Memorize the Quran: How to Choose One That Makes It Stick

Memorizing is easy; keeping it is the challenge. So a memorization app shouldn't be judged by how many surahs it shows, but by whether it brings back what you've memorized before you forget it.

3 min read · Qalqalah team

In this article

  1. What to look for in a memorization app
  2. Tips to make your memorization stick

What to look for in a memorization app

1. A plan that fits your time

A clear daily portion you can actually keep up with beats an ambitious plan you abandon after a week.

2. Spaced review

The most important feature of a memorization app. Spaced review brings verses back at growing intervals: after a day, then a few days, then a week, then a month. You review what you're about to forget, and don't waste time on what's already solid.

3. Nothing new before mastery

New memorization on top of a weak foundation collapses. A good app doesn't move you to a new surah until you've mastered the one before it.

4. Tests that measure mastery

A short test at the end of every unit, and a mastery test for the whole surah, tell you where you really stand.

5. Companions on the way

Memorizing with friends who encourage you and check on you is far easier than memorizing alone.

6. A teacher to recite to

Reciting to someone skilled reveals mistakes you can't see yourself, in your memorization and in your recitation.

Tips to make your memorization stick

  • Correct your recitation before you memorize: what you memorize wrong is hard to fix later. Learn the rules of noon sakinah and meem sakinah early.
  • Memorize from one Mushaf: so the image of each page stays in your memory.
  • Recite what you know in your prayers: it's the best review there is.
  • Put review before new memorization: on a busy day, review and skip the new portion.
  • Listen to a skilled reciter: before and after memorizing.
