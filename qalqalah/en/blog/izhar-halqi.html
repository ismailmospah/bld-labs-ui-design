Izhar Halqi: The Six Throat Letters, with Examples from the Quran

The first rule of noon sakinah and tanween, and the easiest to pronounce: the noon is said clearly, as it is. Knowing its letters precisely is what sets it apart from the other rules.

What is izhar?

Linguistically, izhar means making something clear.

In Tajweed, it means pronouncing the noon sakinah or tanween clearly from its point of articulation, with no added ghunnah, when it is followed by one of the izhar letters.

The six letters of izhar

  • ء
  • ه
  • ع
  • ح
  • غ
  • خ

Hamzah, Ha (ه), Ayn, Ha (ح), Ghayn and Kha

فَالْأَوَّلُ الْإِظْهَارُ قَبْلَ أَحْرُفِلِلْحَلْقِ سِتٍّ رُتِّبَتْ فَلْتَعْرِفِ

هَمْزٌ فَهَاءٌ ثُمَّ عَيْنٌ حَاءُمُهْمَلَتَانِ ثُمَّ غَيْنٌ خَاءُ

“The first is izhar, before the six throat letters in their order, so know them: hamzah, then ha, then ayn and ha (both undotted), then ghayn and kha.”Tuhfat al-Atfal

Why is it called halqi, and why is the noon clear?

It is called halqi (“of the throat”) because its six letters come from the throat. The noon stays clear because its point of articulation, the tip of the tongue, is far from the throat, so the two sounds don't overlap in a way that would call for merging or concealing.

Tip

In «كُلٌّ ءَامَنَ» the tanween on the lam is followed by a hamzah, so it is pronounced clearly. Remember that tanween is pronounced as a noon even though it isn't written as one.

Common mistakes

  • Adding ghunnah: stretching the noon through the nose before the throat letter. It should be pronounced normally and clearly.
  • Pausing on the noon: cutting the sound between it and the throat letter. Join them smoothly.
  • Bouncing the noon: noon is not a qalqalah letter, so it gets no echo.
