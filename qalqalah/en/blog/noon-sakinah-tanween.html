Rules of Noon Sakinah and Tanween: The Complete Guide with Examples

What are noon sakinah and tanween?

Noon sakinah is a noon with no vowel. It appears in nouns, verbs and particles, in the middle or at the end of a word, and it is written and pronounced whether you keep reading or stop, as in «مِنْ», «أَنْعَمْتَ» and «يَنْهَوْنَ».

Tanween is an extra noon sakinah added to the end of a noun in pronunciation. It isn't written as a noon and is dropped when you stop. In the Mushaf it is shown by a doubled vowel: «عَلِيمٌ», «هُدًى», «يَوْمَئِذٍ».

Because tanween is pronounced as a noon sakinah, both follow the same rules, and the rule depends on the letter that comes after them.

لِلنُّونِ إِنْ تَسْكُنْ وَلِلتَّنْوِينِأَرْبَعُ أَحْكَامٍ فَخُذْ تَبْيِينِي

“Noon sakinah and tanween have four rules, so take my explanation.”Tuhfat al-Atfal

1. Izhar halqi (clear pronunciation)

If noon sakinah or tanween is followed by one of the six throat letters, the noon is pronounced clearly, with no added ghunnah.

  • ء
  • ه
  • ع
  • ح
  • غ
  • خ

Hamzah, Ha (ه), Ayn, Ha (ح), Ghayn and Kha

More detail, with an example for each letter: Izhar halqi explained.

2. Idgham (merging)

Idgham means merging the noon sakinah or tanween into the following letter, so the two become one letter with a shaddah. Its six letters are gathered in the word «يَرْمَلُونَ» (yarmaloon), and it comes in two kinds:

Idgham with ghunnah: the letters of «يَنْمُو» (yanmu)

Idgham without ghunnah: lam and ra

Watch out

There is no idgham when the noon and a letter of «يَنْمُو» are in the same word: the noon is pronounced clearly, as in «دُنْيَا», «صِنْوَانٌ», «بُنْيَانٌ» and «قِنْوَانٌ».

The difference between the two kinds, and their exceptions: Idgham with and without ghunnah.

3. Iqlab (conversion)

If noon sakinah or tanween is followed by ba, the noon turns into a concealed meem, with ghunnah. In the Mushaf it is marked by a small meem above the noon or the tanween.

4. Ikhfa haqiqi (concealment)

If noon sakinah or tanween is followed by any of the remaining fifteen letters, the noon is pronounced in a state between izhar and idgham, without a shaddah, while keeping the ghunnah.

  • ص
  • ذ
  • ث
  • ك
  • ج
  • ش
  • ق
  • س
  • د
  • ط
  • ز
  • ف
  • ت
  • ض
  • ظ

How long is the ghunnah?

The ghunnah in idgham with ghunnah, iqlab and ikhfa lasts about two counts (two harakahs). You learn it by listening to a skilled reciter more than by counting.
