How to Learn Tajweed from Scratch: A Practical Plan for Beginners

You don't need any prior knowledge to start. You need the right order, ten minutes a day, and an ear trained on a skilled reciter. Here's a simple plan for your first month.

3 min read · Qalqalah team

  2. The right order to learn in

Why learn Tajweed?

Tajweed means giving every letter its due: its point of articulation, its qualities, and the rules that follow from them. Learning it protects your recitation from mistakes, and brings your reading closer to the way the Quran has been recited and passed down, generation after generation.

The right order to learn in

  1. Points of articulation (makharij): pronouncing each letter from its correct place, especially letters that sound alike, such as ح and هـ, س and ص, ذ and ز and ظ.
  2. The rules of noon sakinah and tanween: the most frequent rules in the Mushaf.
  3. The rules of meem sakinah: three short rules.
  4. Qalqalah: and the qualities of letters in general.
  5. Madd (elongation): its types and lengths.
  6. Practice: on short surahs you know, then reciting to a teacher.

Your first four weeks

WeekWhat to learnPractice on
1Points of articulation, and listening to a skilled reciter every daySurah Al-Fatihah

3Iqlab and ikhfa, then meem sakinahAl-Masad back to Al-Kawthar

Tip

Ten to fifteen minutes a day is enough. Consistency matters more than the length of each session.

Tips for beginners

  • Listen more than you read about the rules: the teaching recitation (al-Mushaf al-Mu'allim) of Sheikh Mahmoud Khalil Al-Husary is one of the most useful things to listen to while learning.
  • One rule at a time: don't move to the next rule until you can recognize the current one when you recite.
  • Look for the rule on every page you read: so Tajweed becomes a habit, not a lesson.
  • Recite to someone skilled: even once a week.

Mistakes beginners make

  • Memorizing rules without applying them: knowing the definition but not spotting the rule in a verse.
  • Exaggerating: stretching the ghunnah or overdoing the qalqalah until the pronunciation changes.
  • Rushing: moving between rules before the first one is solid.
