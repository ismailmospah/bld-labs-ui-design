What Is Qalqalah in Tajweed? Letters, Levels and Examples

Qalqalah is a quality that gives five letters of the Quran a clear echo when they carry a sukun. It is also the name of our app, because it sums up what we love about Tajweed: a small detail that, once mastered, shows in your whole recitation.

What is qalqalah?

Linguistically, qalqalah means disturbance or shaking.

In Tajweed, it is a slight vibration at the letter's point of articulation when the letter is pronounced with a sukun, so that a strong, clear echo is heard. The letter is not swallowed, and it is not given a full vowel either: it gets a light bounce.

The five letters of qalqalah

There are five qalqalah letters, gathered in the phrase «قُطْبُ جَدٍّ» (qutbu jadd). Imam Ibn al-Jazari lists them in his poem, al-Muqaddimah al-Jazariyyah: «قَلْقَلَةٌ قُطْبُ جَدٍّ».

  • ق
  • ط
  • ب
  • ج
  • د

Qaf, Ta, Ba, Jim and Dal

These letters bounce because they combine two strong qualities: jahr (the voice carries without breathiness) and shiddah (the sound is held back). When they carry a sukun, both the sound and the breath are trapped, so the letter needs this echo to be heard.

When does qalqalah happen?

Qalqalah applies to these letters only when they carry a sukun, whether the sukun is part of the word or comes from stopping on the letter. If the letter has a fathah, dammah or kasrah, there is no qalqalah.

The levels of qalqalah

How strong the echo is depends on where the letter with sukun appears:

1. Minor qalqalah (sughra)

When the letter has an original sukun in the middle of a word, or at the end of a word while you keep reading.

2. Major qalqalah (kubra)

When you stop on a qalqalah letter at the end of a word, so it takes a sukun because of the stop. Here the echo is clearer and stronger.

3. A higher level, according to some scholars

Some teachers of recitation mention a third level, stronger than the major one: stopping on a qalqalah letter that has a shaddah, as when stopping on «وَتَبَّ».

Tip

Dividing qalqalah into levels is a teaching convention: some authors give two levels and some three. In practice, what matters is that qalqalah happens on a letter with sukun, and is clearest when you stop on it.

Common mistakes

  • Turning the echo into a full vowel: like reading the qaf of «iqra'» with a fathah. It should be a light bounce, not a vowel.
  • Bouncing a letter that has a vowel: like the qaf in «qul», which has a dammah, so it gets no qalqalah.
  • Exaggerating: until the letter sounds doubled or is followed by an extra sound.
  • Ignoring it: so the letter is swallowed, especially when stopping on «al-falaq» and «ahad».
